# Astronaut Health Risks, Early-Warning Indicators and Autonomous Countermeasures (Exploration Missions)

Scope note: hackathon prototype knowledge base, not medical advice. Anything recommended below needs clinical validation by flight surgeons / NASA HRP before operational use. Research date: 2026-11-14. Coverage is uneven: SANS, CO2, radiation, behavioral health, EIMO and sleep are reasonably sourced; cardiovascular, cognition, hearing/noise, dental/skin/injury and detailed immune items could NOT be sourced in this pass (see Gaps in each section). Web-search summaries were used for some items (flagged "search-snippet only"); verify against the primary document before quoting.

## 1. Which risks rank highest (NASA HRP ratings)?

### Takeaway
A 2020 npj Microgravity review of the HRP "red" risks for a Mars mission names four red risks: space radiation, SANS, behavioral health/performance, and inadequate food/nutrition. Older (2014) HRP charts also rated musculoskeletal, sensorimotor and autonomous medical care red; current ratings were not confirmed and must be checked on the Human Research Roadmap.

### Cited Findings
- Four "red" Mars risks: radiation (cancer, CVD, CNS), SANS, behavioral health and performance, inadequate food and nutrition. Ratings are qualitative LxC (likelihood x consequence), no numeric scores in the paper. — [Red risks for a journey to the red planet, npj Microgravity 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7645687)
- NASA rates each risk by LxC for design reference missions and tracks a path to risk reduction; Mars hazards (radiation, altered gravity, isolation/confinement, closed environment, distance from Earth) link to >30 documented health risks. — [Red risks (ADS record)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020npjMG...6...33P) (search-snippet only)
- A 2014 Multilateral HRP panel chart rated musculoskeletal, sensorimotor and autonomous medical care red for "Mars Now"; this is dated. — [NTRS 20190023004 (search-snippet only)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20190023004/downloads/20190023004.pdf)
- Authoritative current list: [Human Research Roadmap](https://humanresearchroadmap.nasa.gov) (not fetched in this pass).

### Inferences
- For a prototype, prioritizing radiation, SANS, behavioral health, nutrition, plus bone/muscle (historically red) and sleep (cross-cuts all) is defensible. Radiation is mostly not self-manageable beyond shelter procedures; the others are where routine adjustments matter.

### Gaps
- Current (2025-2026) HRP risk ratings not verified; check the roadmap site directly.

## 2. Bone loss and renal stones

### Takeaway
Bone loss in flight is large despite exercise, and bone resorption raises urinary calcium and stone risk; resistive exercise (ARED) plus vitamin D and adequate energy intake is the evidence-backed core, with bisphosphonates as a "consult protocol" option with mixed evidence.

### Cited Findings
- >90% of long-duration crew on Mir/ISS had at least 5% loss at one skeletal site; resorption-driven hypercalciuria increases renal stone risk. — [NTRS 20090041838 (search-snippet only)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20090041838)
- Reduced diuresis and urine acidification add to stone risk in microgravity. — [Aerosp Med Hum Perform review (search-snippet only)](https://meridian-asma-published.prime-prod-local.pubfactory.com/view/journals/amhp/94/12/article-p923.xml)
- ARED resistive exercise combined with vitamin D supplementation protected bone mineral density; raising calcium, fluoride or phosphate alone was not effective; high protein, sodium and iron intake are detrimental to bone, fish intake beneficial. — [npj Microgravity 2020 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7645687)
- Bisphosphonates (e.g., alendronate 70 mg/week in small samples) show promise for blunting bone loss, and a 2022 JBMR Plus paper suggests reduced urolithiasis risk, but results are mixed and the class has side effects. — [JBMR Plus 2022](https://doaj.org/article/c0cc6bd310384128956e07d370d2604b); [PubMed 35079672](https://www.pubmed.gov/35079672/); [JAXA bisphosphonate study](https://iss.jaxa.jp/en/kiboexp/theme/first/bisphosphonates/)
- On 180-day missions about 2-7% BMD drop despite ~2.5 h/day exercise (secondary summary). — [SPRINT paper search result](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7434884/)
- The npj review's reference list names sodium chloride intake (worsened immobilization-induced resorption), dietary acid load, omega-3 and short-term high calcium (no effect on turnover markers during bed rest) as studied factors; titles only, no data read. — [npj Microgravity 2020 refs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7645687)

### Inferences
- Candidate onboard signals: urine volume/specific gravity (hydration), urine pH, body mass trend, missed/shortened ARED sessions, flank pain/hematuria symptom check. Candidate routine changes: protect ARED sessions, raise fluid intake when urine output is low, limit sodium. Escalation: flank pain, hematuria, or fever with stone symptoms -> alert CMO immediately.

### Gaps
- No sourced numeric hydration target (urine volume) or potassium citrate evidence found; no NASA-STD-3001 bone numbers retrieved. Onboard bone density measurement (ultrasound/DXA) not covered.

## 3. Muscle atrophy and aerobic deconditioning

### Takeaway
Multisystem deconditioning occurs even with ISS exercise; the NASA SPRINT study showed a shorter, high-intensity/lower-volume regimen gave similar outcomes to standard training, so its value is mainly crew-time saving.

### Cited Findings
- SPRINT: high-intensity/lower-volume vs standard training; weekly resistance volume load 34-44% lower and aerobic volume 17% lower in SPRINT; no group x time interactions; standard group still showed decrements in muscle mass, strength, function, cardiorespiratory fitness and BMD. — [NASA SPRINT study, PMC7434884 (search-snippet only)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7434884/)
- 180-day missions: 11-17% strength decrease, ~10% endurance decrease (secondary summary). — [same source](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7434884/)
- 2022 work extends SPRINT with a flywheel device and exergaming for beyond-LEO missions. — [Frontiers in Physiology 2022 (search-snippet only)](https://frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2022.932425/pdf)
- ISS hardware upgraded in 2009 (second-generation treadmill, ARED). — [same SPRINT source](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7434884/)
- WVU/NASA-funded digital-twin work models movement and muscle activation to predict neuromuscular decline (trade-press summary). — [Pulse](https://www.pulse.bot/ai/news/ai-digital-twins-aim-to-protect-astronaut-mobility-on-deep-space-missions-23e8f18b-4d36-4a3c-b7ef-0abbccb78159/)

### Inferences
- Signals: exercise log adherence (sessions, load, HR), ARED load progression as strength proxy, body mass, submaximal HR at fixed workload as VO2 proxy. Self-care: maintain/restore prescribed sessions; shift to short high-intensity sessions when time is constrained.

### Gaps
- VO2max decline numbers, NASA-STD-3001 V2 4015 (aerobic capacity) text and numeric fitness thresholds not retrieved (PMC page blocked by reCAPTCHA).

## 4. SANS and fluid shifts (headache, vision, CO2)

### Takeaway
SANS is red-rated, unresolved in cause, can start by flight day 10, and has NASA-defined imaging thresholds; space anticipation glasses are the only proven countermeasure (for refractive shift), and LBNP has the most supporting data but mostly from ground analogs. CO2's role in SANS is unresolved but well established for headaches.

### Cited Findings
- Incidence after long-duration flight: clinically significant optic disc edema (Frisen >=1) ~12-19%, >=20 um peripapillary thickening ~70%, chorioretinal folds up to ~20%, globe flattening ~23%; structural changes can appear by flight day 10; near-vision difficulty 48% after long vs 23% after short flights. — [SANS review, PMC12103276](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12103276)
- NASA "earliest indication" thresholds: delta total retinal thickness >=20 um; "clinically concerning" >=55 um and/or Frisen >=1; any choroidal/retinal/peripapillary folds; refractive shift >=+0.75 D (>=+1.50 D near the limit of in-flight anticipation glasses). — [PMC12103276](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12103276)
- Predisposing factors proposed: smaller optic cup, longer axial length, one-carbon metabolism variants (MTRR, SHMT1), short sleep. — [PMC12103276](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12103276)
- ISS CO2 ~3 mmHg vs ~1 mmHg on Earth; 1% CO2 during head-down tilt did not worsen optic nerve sheath diameter; CO2 contribution to SANS unresolved. — [PMC12103276](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12103276)
- LBNP typically 20-30 mmHg; in space 25 mmHg for 30 min restored jugular/splanchnic engorgement toward preflight; 10-20 min lowered IOP toward upright levels; nightly ~8 h at 20 mmHg reduced choroidal expansion in analogs; effects often transient; adverse effects include LBNP-induced syncope. — [PMC12103276](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12103276); [IAC 2024 abstract (search-snippet only)](https://iafastro.directory/iac/paper/id/81093/abstract-pdf/IAC-24,A1,3,5,x81093.brief.pdf)
- Other countermeasures under study: thigh cuffs, impedance threshold devices, translaminar pressure modulation, centrifugation/artificial gravity, pharmaceuticals, B-vitamin supplementation (hypothesis, not validated). — [Progress in Retinal and Eye Research 2025 review (search-snippet only)](https://synapse.mskcc.org/synapse/works/223848); [npj Microgravity 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7645687)
- CO2 and headache (NASA-STD-3001 V2 [V2 6004], OCHMO TB-004 Rev D): average 1-h ppCO2 <= 3 mmHg; to keep headache risk <1%, 7-day average <2.5 mmHg; headaches at 2.8-4.5 mmHg; fatigue, decreased sleep, nausea above 4.5 mmHg; blurred vision and frontal headaches reported at 3.5 mmHg; 19 of 49 astronauts (~38.7%) reported headaches; ISS range 1-9 mmHg. — [OCHMO TB-004 Carbon Dioxide](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/ochmo-tb-004-carbon-dioxide.pdf); [NASA-STD-3001 Vol 2 (historical)](https://standards.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/standards/NASA/B/0/Historical/nasa-std-3001_vol_2.pdf)
- The limit was lowered from earlier 3.8-7.5 mmHg values; an older brief cites Rev C and the newer Rev D for the same 3 mmHg requirement. — [OCHMO CO2 brief (older)](https://nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/co2-technical-brief-ochmo.pdf)
- Basner et al. 2017 HDT study at 0.5% CO2 (=3.8 mmHg): physical exhaustion and mental fatigue increased with exposure duration. — [OCHMO TB-004](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/ochmo-tb-004-carbon-dioxide.pdf)

### Inferences
- Onboard signals: daily headache/vision questionnaire, near-vision test, ppCO2 sensor readings per module (including pockets near sleep stations), periodic OCT/ultrasound (optic nerve sheath), tonometry. Possible app rule: 7-day mean ppCO2 >=2.5 mmHg plus headache reports -> suggest ventilation/relocation and flag. New blurred near vision or a >=0.75 D shift -> escalate to ground/CMO; do not auto-diagnose.
- Evidence for lifestyle routines against SANS is low; the actionable parts are CO2 control and tracking.

### Gaps
- Evidence strength for thigh cuffs, ITD, vitamin supplements not read (review text truncated). No validated in-flight LBNP prescription. OCT/Frisen grading requires trained imaging; self-administration not covered.

## 5. Cardiovascular changes, orthostatic intolerance, arrhythmia

### Takeaway
Little sourced material was obtained. Only radiation-related CVD risk and the wearable ECG/heart-rate monitoring concept are sourced.

### Cited Findings
- Circulatory disease could raise radiation exposure-induced death risk roughly 40% over cancer alone (preliminary); CVD latency >=10 years at <0.5 Gy; endothelial dysfunction a key event. — [npj Microgravity 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7645687)
- Autonomy Level 0 monitoring is basic ECG/heart rate; ESA MagIC-Space vest tracked ECG, respiration, temperature, movement during sleep. — [SpaceCHI 2025](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/01oasics/oasics-vol130-spacechi2025/html/OASIcs.SpaceCHI.2025.33/OASIcs.SpaceCHI.2025.33.html)
- Fluid shifts, microgravity and sensor-skin adhesion changes shift baselines and add artifacts. — [SpaceCHI 2025](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/01oasics/oasics-vol130-spacechi2025/html/OASIcs.SpaceCHI.2025.33/OASIcs.SpaceCHI.2025.33.html)

### Inferences
- Orthostatic risk is likely highest on return to gravity (landing or Mars arrival), so an app could schedule pre-arrival routines; unsourced, needs a protocol from NASA sources.

### Gaps
- Orthostatic intolerance prevalence, countermeasures (fluid loading, compression garments), arrhythmia incidence and NASA-STD-3001 cardiac numbers not found in this pass.

## 6. Sleep loss, circadian misalignment, fatigue

### Takeaway
Crew average roughly 6 hours of sleep versus 8.5 scheduled, and circadian misalignment cuts sleep further; lighting schedules (blue-depleted evening, blue-enriched morning) are the studied countermeasure but outcome data was not retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- Average in-flight sleep 5.96 h (shuttle) and 6.09 h (ISS) despite 8.5 h scheduled. — [Lancet Neurology release via EurekAlert (search-snippet only)](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/541449)
- Analysis of 21 ISS astronauts: 6.4 +/- 1.2 h aligned vs 5.4 +/- 1.4 h misaligned sleep (exact source URL not confirmed; see related [PNAS Basner 2013](https://www.med.upenn.edu/uep/assets/user-content/documents/PNAS2013Basner1212646110withSupportingAppendix.pdf)).
- ISS solid-state lighting trial protocol: three blue-depleted white settings before bed, high-intensity blue-enriched light in the morning; outcomes sleep duration/efficiency via actigraphy and logs. Results not found. — [NTRS 20160006727](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20160006727/downloads/20160006727.pdf)
- Short sleep is listed as a possible SANS predisposing factor. — [PMC12103276](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12103276)
- CO2 above 4.5 mmHg associated with decreased sleep. — [OCHMO TB-004](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/ochmo-tb-004-carbon-dioxide.pdf)

### Inferences
- Signals: wearable/actigraphy sleep duration and efficiency vs personal baseline, sleep timing vs mission clock, sleepiness questionnaire. Routine adjustments: fixed sleep window, evening dim/blue-depleted light, morning bright light, check ppCO2 near sleep quarters. Escalation thresholds not found; need flight-surgeon definition.

### Gaps
- No NASA-STD-3001 numeric sleep requirements; hypnotic medication guidance not covered; Mars sol schedule adaptation not researched.

## 7. Behavioral health, isolation and team conflict

### Takeaway
Rated red; analog data show symptom clustering and uneven conflict distribution; no validated Mars-length countermeasure exists.

### Cited Findings
- ISS countermeasures: private psychological conferences, care packages; HERA analog up to 45 days. No validated countermeasure for Mars-length missions. — [npj Microgravity 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7645687)
- "Third-quarter" morale dip suggested by ISS journal analyses. — [npj Microgravity 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7645687)
- 520-day Mars simulation: one of six crew reported depressive symptoms in 93% of weeks; BDI mild-moderate level reached in >10% of weeks; two highest-stress crew accounted for 85% of perceived conflicts. — [npj Microgravity 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7645687)
- Delays and lack of ground support increase task demand, task switching and cognitive load, which impair decision making. — [EIMO ConOps 2024](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20240001402/downloads/2024_IWS_EIMO_ConOps_FINAL.pdf)

### Inferences
- Signals: weekly mood questionnaires, communication frequency, sleep, workload; flag trends rather than absolute scores. Mission-phase prompts (third quarter) could pre-empt dips. Team-level signals are sensitive; design for privacy.

### Gaps
- Validated instruments, thresholds and delayed-communication-specific interventions not retrieved.

## 8. Cognitive performance

### Takeaway
Little sourced material on in-flight cognitive decline (e.g., Cognition test battery results). Only indirect links found.

### Cited Findings
- Radiation CNS effects are acute and may include cognitive, motor and neurobehavioral changes; rodent dendritic spine density reduced at 5 and 30 cGy (16O). — [npj Microgravity 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7645687)
- CO2 at 0.5% (3.8 mmHg) increased mental fatigue during HDT. — [OCHMO TB-004](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/ochmo-tb-004-carbon-dioxide.pdf)
- Inspiration4 SOMA data reported brain proteins in blood, suggesting possible blood-brain barrier disruption (press release). — [Weill Cornell](https://news.weill.cornell.edu/news/2024/06/commercial-astronauts-shed-light-on-flights%E2%80%99-health-impacts-and-create-spaceflight)

### Gaps
- Cognition battery results and fatigue-related error thresholds not found.

## 9. Space radiation

### Takeaway
Limits are dose based; the onboard actions are dosimetry tracking and solar particle event shelter, not behavioral routines.

### Cited Findings
- Career limit [V1 4030]: total effective dose <600 mSv, universal for ages and sexes, derived from mean 3% REID for a 35-year-old female; short-term SPE limit cited as 250 mSv. Revision wording differs across documents; verify current revision. — [NASA radiation protection technical brief](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/radiation-protection-technical-brief-ochmo.pdf) (search-snippet only); [NASEM 2021](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/26155/chapter/5)
- Typical absorbed doses: ISS 6-12 months ~30-120 mGy; lunar surface 1 year ~100-120 mGy; Mars ~3 years ~300-450 mGy. — [npj Microgravity 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7645687)
- No validated radiation medical countermeasures; no clinically significant radiation-related deficits identified in ISS astronauts. — [npj Microgravity 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7645687)

### Inferences
- App can track cumulative dosimeter readings against the career limit and prompt shelter procedures on SPE alerts; acute prodromal symptoms (nausea/vomiting) would warrant immediate escalation (unsourced clinical judgment).

### Gaps
- SPE shelter procedures and dose-rate triggers not retrieved.

## 10. Immune dysregulation, latent virus reactivation

### Takeaway
SOMA/Inspiration4 and earlier work show immune changes and herpesvirus reactivation in roughly half of space travelers (press-release level figure).

### Cited Findings
- About half of space travelers/astronauts showed reactivation of old viral infections (e.g., HSV-1), plus a cytokine spike after reentry. — [Weill Cornell, SOMA 2024](https://news.weill.cornell.edu/news/2024/06/commercial-astronauts-shed-light-on-flights%E2%80%99-health-impacts-and-create-spaceflight)
- Nature SOMA overview: Overbey et al., Nature 2024, DOI 10.1038/s41586-024-07639-y; an Inspiration4 Nature Microbiology paper reports immune suppression and latent herpesvirus reactivation. — [PMC11222149](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11222149)
- Vitamin D supplementation during Antarctic winter may mitigate EBV reactivation (BMI related). — [Zwart 2011, cited in npj review refs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7645687)

### Inferences
- Candidate signals: symptom logs (cold sores, rash), temperature, stress and sleep trends as drivers. Evidence for lifestyle countermeasures is thin.

### Gaps
- Primary-paper percentages, shedding thresholds and infection countermeasures not read.

## 11. Nutrition deficits, body mass, food system

### Takeaway
Rated red; body mass loss tracks with bone/muscle/CV decline, and fresh food supply ends on Mars missions.

### Cited Findings
- Closed food system raises deficiency risk; body mass loss linked to bone and muscle loss, CV decline and oxidative stress; fluid is only ~1% of body mass loss. — [npj Microgravity 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7645687)
- Fresh produce arrives on ISS about every 1-2 months; exploration missions have zero consumables resupply. — [npj Microgravity 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7645687); [EIMO ConOps](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20240001402/downloads/2024_IWS_EIMO_ConOps_FINAL.pdf)
- Targeted B-vitamin supplementation proposed for SANS (hypothesis). — [npj Microgravity 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7645687)

### Inferences
- Signals: weekly body mass (onboard mass measurement), food intake logs. Numeric weight-loss alert thresholds not found and must be set clinically.

### Gaps
- NASA-STD-3001 energy/nutrient requirements and food degradation data not retrieved.

## 12. Hearing loss/noise and CO2 symptoms

### Takeaway
CO2 symptom thresholds are well documented (section 4); noise limits were not found.

### Cited Findings
- CO2 toxicity symptoms: headache, dyspnea, fatigue; the two most common on ISS are headaches and vision changes; 5th cranial nerve dysesthesia at 4.5-5.0 mmHg. — [OCHMO TB-004](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/ochmo-tb-004-carbon-dioxide.pdf)
- Earth indoor air is ~0.608-0.76 mmHg CO2. — [OCHMO TB-004](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/ochmo-tb-004-carbon-dioxide.pdf)

### Gaps
- Noise exposure limits and hearing threshold shift criteria from NASA-STD-3001: no source found.

## 13. Medical events requiring autonomy and the EIMO concept

### Takeaway
EIMO is the gradual transition of medical care and decision making from terrestrial to space-based assets; Mars-class missions have no real-time comms, blackouts, no evacuation and no resupply.

### Cited Findings
- EIMO definition; ISS current state (180-360 day missions, resupply, real-time comms, evacuation possible) vs exploration (650 to >900 days, zero resupply, no real-time comms plus blackouts, no sample returns, no evacuation, smaller volume). — [EIMO ConOps, HRP IWS Feb 2024](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20240001402/downloads/2024_IWS_EIMO_ConOps_FINAL.pdf)
- EIMO elements: pre-mission planning (on-board knowledge/skills, just-in-time training), acute/emergent management with an on-board Clinical Decision Support System (CDSS), prolonged medical decision making, supplies management, task load management; system of systems using multiple data streams and AI/ML recommendations; AMO project developing an on-board Medical Decision Support System. — [EIMO ConOps](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20240001402/downloads/2024_IWS_EIMO_ConOps_FINAL.pdf)
- Other EIMO/ExMC records (abstracts only seen via search): [NTRS 20250000009](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20250000009), [NTRS 20220007443](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20220007443), [NTRS 20220017885](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20220017885).

### Inferences
- A preventive engine fits EIMO's stated aim to monitor and maintain crew health and performance during communication delays.

### Gaps
- Medical event incidence tables, and dental/skin/injury data, not retrieved. Stone mechanism is in section 2.

## 14. How communication delay changes medical decision-making

### Takeaway
Mars one-way delay of 4-24 minutes, hours to no contact deeper out, plus conjunction blackouts: real-time telemedicine does not work, so crew and onboard systems must self-manage and prevention matters.

### Cited Findings
- Four autonomy levels: L0 ISS (0.03-0.25 s one-way, return 3-6 h); L1 Moon (1.2-2.6 s, return 3-5 days); L2 Mars (4-24 min, return 6-9 months or >1 year); L3 deep space (hours to none, no return) where onboard systems handle diagnostics, intervention and preventive care. — [SpaceCHI 2025](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/01oasics/oasics-vol130-spacechi2025/html/OASIcs.SpaceCHI.2025.33/OASIcs.SpaceCHI.2025.33.html)
- Exploration missions have communication latency and reduced data bandwidth; non-emergent care should depend less on real-time ground support. — [EIMO ConOps](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20240001402/downloads/2024_IWS_EIMO_ConOps_FINAL.pdf)

### Gaps
- Solar conjunction blackout duration and ExMC requirement documents beyond the ConOps not retrieved.

## 15. Anomaly detection approaches and alarm fatigue

### Takeaway
Published direction is personalized baselines plus deviation detection, explainable and uncertainty-aware outputs; no source retrieved on Bayesian/online change-point methods or alarm fatigue in astronauts.

### Cited Findings
- 27 astronauts' molecular and physiological measurements over long-duration flights analyzed with spectral methods; authors call for personalized baselines and deviation detection. — [Zheng et al., Frontiers in Physiology 2023 (search-snippet only)](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1219221/pdf)
- Portland State (NASA-funded) system learns each crew member's physiology and updates the baseline as the body adapts, flagging deviations relative to the individual. — [PSU news](https://www.pdx.edu/news/when-911-millions-miles-away-psu-researcher-builds-ai-guardian-mars-missions)
- WVU digital twins of movement/muscle activation to predict neuromuscular decline. — [Pulse](https://www.pulse.bot/ai/news/ai-digital-twins-aim-to-protect-astronaut-mobility-on-deep-space-missions-23e8f18b-4d36-4a3c-b7ef-0abbccb78159/)
- Design guidance: show uncertainty/confidence, flag incomplete input, walk the crew through verification when a sensor anomaly occurs, prioritize alerts at the edge, context-aware filtering, adaptive calibration, redundant sensing; the paper gives no alarm thresholds or alarm-fatigue guidance. — [SpaceCHI 2025](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/01oasics/oasics-vol130-spacechi2025/html/OASIcs.SpaceCHI.2025.33/OASIcs.SpaceCHI.2025.33.html)
- CDSS value paper (npj Microgravity 2023): abstract only seen. — [catalog record](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edsdoj,edsdoj.3d354e4a472c41389e698cd46b56a2c8)

### Inferences
- Because microgravity shifts baselines and adds sensor artifacts, a prototype could use per-person adaptive baselines, require persistence across days, and combine at least two signals before alerting (design choices, not cited findings).

### Gaps
- No sourced results for Bayesian online change-point or CUSUM in astronaut data, nor alarm-fatigue lessons in space; terrestrial clinical alarm-fatigue literature was not searched.
