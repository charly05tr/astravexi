## 3. Ongoing HRP research relevant to monitoring/countermeasures (SRC: https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/ongoing-hrp-research/)

Artemis II
- ARCHeR: tracks crew well-being, activity and sleep. https://www.nasa.gov/reference/archer/
- Immune Biomarkers: crew blood and saliva to monitor immune changes. https://www.nasa.gov/reference/immune-biomarkers/
- Artemis II Standard Measures: consistent crew health data for a shared data bank. https://www.nasa.gov/reference/artemis-ii-standard-measures/

Low Earth Orbit
- B-Complex (countermeasure): daily B vitamin to prevent/reduce brain and eye changes. https://www.nasa.gov/reference/b-complex/
- CIPHER: studies tracking changes across multiple body systems. https://www.nasa.gov/reference/cipher/
- Spacecraft Occupant Protection: re-entry discomfort surveys plus spacecraft sensor data. https://www.nasa.gov/reference/spacecraft-occupant-protection/
- Standard Measures: consistent physiological and psychological measurements. https://www.nasa.gov/reference/standard-measures/
- Thigh Cuff (countermeasure): tight thigh cuffs to keep fluid in legs and help prevent eye disorders. https://www.nasa.gov/reference/thigh-cuff/
- Zero T2 (countermeasure): exercise in space without a treadmill. https://www.nasa.gov/reference/zero-t2/

Past investigations
- Altered Immunity: blood, saliva and urine. https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/scientists-probe-how-long-term-spaceflight-alters-immunity/
- Food Physiology (countermeasure): enhanced diet. https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/enhanced-diet-may-help-astronauts-adapt-to-spaceflight/
- Omics Archive: multiple sample types for molecular-level tracking. https://www.nasa.gov/reference/omics-archive/
- Space Gardening (countermeasure): fresh vegetables vs isolation. https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/can-space-gardening-help-astronauts-cope-with-isolation/
- Spinal Health (countermeasure): spine scans to inform bone/muscle loss strategies. https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/astronauts-spines-under-scrutiny/
- Tempus Pro: telemedicine device integrating health data for near real-time diagnosis. https://www.nasa.gov/reference/tempus-pro/
- Veggie Monitoring: microbial growth on crop-storage hardware. https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/researchers-put-space-garden-microbes-under-the-microscope/

## 4. CSA resources

Article "Monitoring radiation for astronaut health" (SRC: https://www.asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/news/articles/2026/2026-02-26-monitoring-radiation-astronaut-health.asp). It covers radiation technologies only; it does not mention Bio-Monitor.
- CANS (Canadian Active Neutron Spectrometer): compact, autonomous instrument for measuring neutron radiation exposure; CSA contract of CAD 5.5 million to Bubble Technology Industries for continued development (same URL; amount from search excerpt of the article).
- Bubble detectors: predecessor to CANS; photo of David Saint-Jacques using them on the ISS (same URL). Commissioned 1988, first flown 1989 per search snippet of a CSA page (not fetched): https://www.asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/astronauts/space-medicine/detector.asp
- MOSFET dosimeter: small silicon sensor measuring absorbed radiation; shielding of different thicknesses lets it estimate dose to skin, eyes and blood-forming organs. https://www.asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/astronauts/space-medicine/mosfet.asp
- EVARM (ExtraVehicular Activity Radiation Monitor): MOSFET version used on the ISS to record individual exposure during spacewalks (same MOSFET page; article link /eng/iss/evarm.asp).
- Radi-N / Radi-N2: neutron dose to astronauts on the ISS using bubble monitors (polymer gel tubes with droplets that vaporize into countable bubbles when hit by neutrons). https://www.asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/sciences/radi-n2.asp
- Related pages listed by the article (not fetched): Testing Technologies /eng/iss/testing-technologies.asp; Experiments on the station /eng/iss/science/experiments.asp

Article "Why do we conduct science experiments in space?" (SRC: https://asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/iss/science/why-do-we-conduct-science-experiments-in-space.asp)
- Rationale: prepare for deep-space missions and benefit life on Earth; longer Moon/Mars missions add radiation, isolation and communication delay; weightlessness mimics "accelerated aging" and sedentary effects, relevant to balance problems, osteoporosis, cardiovascular disorders and type 2 diabetes; about 200 international experiments aboard the ISS at any time; research covers bones, heart, blood vessels and brain plus countermeasures. Names no specific monitoring tools.

Bio-Monitor (SRC: search results, pages not opened): wearable system with smart shirt, headband and tablet app measuring heart rate, respiration rate and blood oxygen; first used on the ISS by David Saint-Jacques. https://www.asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/multimedia/search/video/18224 ; related studies Space Health https://www.asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/sciences/space-flight-improve-monitor-health-earth.asp and CARDIOBREATH (blood pressure regulation) https://www.asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/sciences/cardiobreath.asp . Existing report also cites Astroskin: https://asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/sciences/astroskin.asp
